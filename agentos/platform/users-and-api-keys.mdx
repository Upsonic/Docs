## Tenants and environments

AgentOS is organised in three levels:

- A **tenant** is your organisation. Everything you do happens inside it, and tenants never see each other.
- A tenant holds **environments** (`default`, `staging`, `prod`…). Each environment has its own agents, models, guardrails, secrets, data and roles.
- Every **user** and **service account** belongs to exactly one tenant. Someone who works for two tenants has two accounts.

If you have a role in two or more environments, the tenant block at the bottom of the left rail becomes a menu: use **Switch environment** to move between them.

## Roles

A role is given **per environment**. Without one you have no access to that environment.

| Role | Can |
|---|---|
| **Member** | Run agents, read runs, holds, memory and logs, decide holds the capability allows members to decide, work on evals, and see the configuration (guardrails, network rules, secret names, webhook endpoints). |
| **Admin** | Everything a member can, plus deploy and roll back agents, manage providers and models, guardrails, network rules, secrets and webhooks, delete and erase memory, and read the [audit log](/agentos/platform/audit-log). |

A **tenant admin** manages the tenant's users, service accounts and API keys, and creates environments. Being tenant admin opens no environment by itself: a tenant admin also needs a role in each environment they work in. Whoever creates an environment becomes its admin.

## Manage people

Tenant admins open **Users** from the avatar menu (top right). The page shows the tenant's users and service accounts.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add a user">
    Click **Add user**, enter their **Email** and a **Temporary password**. The password isn't sent anywhere: give it to the person yourself.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Give roles">
    Set their **Role in** each environment (**No access**, **Member** or **Admin**), and whether they are a **Tenant admin**. Click **Save roles** to change them later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="They change their password">
    After signing in with the temporary password, they choose their own under **avatar menu → Change password**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

From a user's page a tenant admin can also:

- **Reset password**: set a new temporary password. This signs the person out everywhere.
- **Deactivate**: they can't sign in until reactivated, and their sessions end. Their roles are kept. **Reactivate** lets them sign in again with their current password.

There is no delete: users are deactivated instead.

### New environments

Tenant admins create environments from **Users → New environment**. You become the new environment's admin; give others roles on their user pages.

## Service accounts and API keys

Scripts, CI pipelines, schedulers and your own backends use a **service account**: an account with roles and API keys, which can't sign in to the panel.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add a service account">
    On **Users**, click **Add service account**. Give it a **Name** (lowercase letters, digits and hyphens, such as `github-ci`) and a **Description** of what uses it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Give it roles">
    Give it a role in each environment it calls: **Member** to run agents and decide holds, **Admin** to also deploy.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a key">
    Click **Create key**. The key (`aos_…`) is shown **once**: copy it now. If nobody copied it, revoke it and create another.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Send the key as a bearer token on any `/v0` request:

```bash
curl -sS -H "Authorization: Bearer $AGENTOS_API_KEY" $AGENTOS/v0/envs/default/agents/hello-agent
```

No CSRF token or cookie is needed with a key. See the [API overview](/agentos/api/overview).

The service account's page lists its keys with when each was created and last used.

- **Revoke** a key: requests that send it are refused at once. A revoked key can't be brought back; whoever used it needs a new one.
- **Deactivate** the service account: all its keys stop working until it is reactivated. Its roles and keys are kept, and keys that weren't revoked work again after **Reactivate**.

<Warning>
Never put an API key in a browser or a mobile app. Call AgentOS from your backend, and let the backend pass results (or a [passthrough stream](/agentos/api/runs)) to the browser.
</Warning>

Signing in, failed sign-ins, user and role changes, and key creation and revocation are all recorded in the [audit log](/agentos/platform/audit-log).
